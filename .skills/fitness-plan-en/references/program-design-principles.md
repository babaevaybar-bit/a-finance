# Program Design Principles

Use this reference when creating detailed fitness plans. Adapt all recommendations to the user's level, schedule, equipment, recovery, and safety constraints.

## Safety Screening

Before creating demanding plans, flag the need for professional clearance when the user reports:

- Chest pain, fainting, unexplained severe shortness of breath, or sudden dizziness.
- Recent surgery, acute injury, fracture, or severe joint pain.
- Pregnancy or early postpartum period.
- Uncontrolled hypertension, heart disease, diabetes complications, neurological symptoms, or significant chronic illness.
- Eating disorder history or extreme weight-loss goals.
- Medication or medical condition that affects exercise tolerance.

For low-risk users, use conservative progression and advise stopping for sharp pain or concerning symptoms.

## Goal Selection

Common goals:

- General health: consistency, full-body strength, cardio base, mobility.
- Fat loss: resistance training, steps/cardio, habit consistency, recovery.
- Hypertrophy: weekly muscle volume, proximity to failure, exercise stability.
- Strength: specific lifts, heavier loads, lower reps, longer rest.
- Endurance: aerobic base, gradual volume, one or two quality sessions.
- Mobility: frequent low-intensity practice, range control, joint-specific work.
- Return to training: lower volume, slower progression, soreness management.

If the user has multiple goals, rank them:

1. Safety and adherence.
2. Primary outcome.
3. Secondary outcome.
4. Nice-to-have preferences.

## Training Frequency

Default frequency guidance:

| Level | Strength / resistance training | Cardio | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Beginner | 2 to 3 days/week | 2 to 4 easy sessions/week | Full-body plans work well. |
| Returning | 2 to 4 days/week | 2 to 4 sessions/week | Start below previous peak. |
| Intermediate | 3 to 5 days/week | 2 to 5 sessions/week | Use upper/lower, full-body, or push/pull/legs. |
| Advanced | 4 to 6 days/week | Goal-dependent | Requires recovery management and planned variation. |

General health anchor:

- Aim for regular aerobic activity and at least two muscle-strengthening sessions per week when feasible.
- Spread training through the week instead of cramming all hard work into one or two days.

## Session Structure

Use this order unless the user's goal suggests otherwise:

1. Readiness check: pain, sleep, soreness, energy.
2. General warm-up: 5 to 10 minutes.
3. Specific warm-up: movement pattern ramp-up.
4. Main lift or primary skill.
5. Secondary strength or hypertrophy work.
6. Accessories, core, carries, or conditioning.
7. Cool-down or mobility.
8. Log results and notes.

## Strength Programming

Best for maximal force, barbell skill, and measurable lift improvement.

Typical structure:

- Main lifts: 2 to 5 sets of 1 to 6 reps.
- Accessories: 2 to 4 sets of 6 to 12 reps.
- Rest: 2 to 5 minutes for heavy compound lifts.
- Effort: usually RPE 7 to 9, leaving 1 to 3 reps in reserve.
- Progression: add small load, reps, or sets when technique and recovery are stable.

Beginner strength:

- Use simple full-body or upper/lower plans.
- Keep reps moderate while learning technique.
- Avoid frequent max testing.

## Hypertrophy Programming

Best for muscle gain and body recomposition.

Typical structure:

- 6 to 20 reps per set.
- 2 to 5 sets per exercise.
- 8 to 16 challenging sets per muscle group per week for many users, adjusted by level and recovery.
- Rest: 1 to 3 minutes depending on exercise difficulty.
- Effort: most working sets within 1 to 3 reps in reserve.
- Progression: add reps first, then load; maintain control and range of motion.

Common splits:

- 3 days: full body.
- 4 days: upper/lower.
- 5 to 6 days: push/pull/legs or body-part emphasis.

## Fat-Loss and Body Recomposition Training

Training priorities:

1. Resistance training to preserve or build muscle.
2. Daily activity such as steps or low-intensity movement.
3. Moderate cardio for health and energy expenditure.
4. Recovery to maintain adherence.

Avoid:

- Excessive HIIT for beginners.
- Very high training volume with low sleep.
- Framing exercise as punishment for eating.

Plan format:

- 2 to 4 resistance sessions/week.
- 2 to 5 cardio or walking sessions/week.
- 1 to 2 rest or active-recovery days/week.
- Sustainable habit target such as steps, meal prep, or bedtime.

## Cardio and Endurance Programming

Use simple intensity zones when exact heart rate data is missing:

- Easy: can speak in full sentences.
- Moderate: can speak in short phrases.
- Hard: difficult to speak; use sparingly.

Beginner running/cardio:

- Start with walk-run or low-impact cardio.
- Increase total weekly time gradually.
- Keep most sessions easy.
- Add intensity only after consistency is established.

Common weekly structure:

- Base building: 3 to 5 easy sessions.
- General fitness: 2 to 4 easy/moderate sessions.
- Performance: mostly easy work plus 1 to 2 quality sessions.

## Mobility and Flexibility Programming

Use for stiffness, range of motion, warm-ups, or recovery.

Principles:

- Use short, frequent practice.
- Pair mobility with strength through the new range.
- Do not force painful ranges.
- Match mobility work to the user's sport, lifts, posture demands, or daily discomfort.

Plan:

- Daily 5 to 10 minute mobility snack, or
- 2 to 4 dedicated 20 minute sessions/week.

## Progressive Overload

Ways to progress:

- Add reps.
- Add load.
- Add sets.
- Increase range of motion.
- Slow tempo.
- Reduce rest slightly.
- Use harder variation.
- Add weekly frequency.

Simple double progression:

```text
Choose a rep range, such as 8-12.
When all sets reach the top of the range with good form and target effort, increase load slightly next time.
Return to the lower end of the rep range and build again.
```

Progress only when:

- Technique is stable.
- Pain is absent or manageable and non-worsening.
- Recovery is adequate.
- The user completed the planned work.

## Deloads and Recovery

Use deloads when:

- Performance drops for multiple sessions.
- Soreness persists.
- Motivation and sleep decline.
- Joint irritation grows.
- A hard 4 to 8 week block ends.

Deload options:

- Reduce volume by 30% to 50%.
- Keep movements but reduce load.
- Replace hard conditioning with easy cardio.
- Emphasize mobility and technique.

Recovery basics:

- Sleep.
- Protein and overall food adequacy.
- Hydration.
- Rest days.
- Stress management.
- Avoiding unnecessary failure training.

## Readiness and Adjustment Rules

Use this traffic-light system:

- Green: normal energy, no unusual pain, soreness manageable. Follow plan.
- Yellow: poor sleep, high stress, lingering soreness, mild aches. Reduce load or volume by 10% to 30%.
- Red: sharp pain, illness, chest pain, dizziness, acute injury. Stop or switch to rest and seek appropriate help.

## Common Mistakes

- Too much intensity too soon.
- No planned progression.
- No rest days.
- Changing exercises every session.
- Ignoring pain signals.
- Training only preferred muscles or movements.
- Mixing too many goals equally.
- Using advanced splits before building consistency.
