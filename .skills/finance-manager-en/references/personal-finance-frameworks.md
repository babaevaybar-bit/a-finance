# Personal Finance Frameworks

Use these frameworks as defaults, not rigid rules. Adjust for local cost of living, household size, income volatility, health needs, dependents, debt obligations, and user goals.

## Core Financial Health Indicators

| Area | Healthy Pattern | Risk Pattern |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cash flow | Income consistently exceeds expenses. | Expenses exceed income or require debt to cover basics. |
| Savings rate | User saves a meaningful share of income. | Savings rate is near zero or negative. |
| Emergency fund | Liquid savings can cover essential expenses. | User has no buffer for income loss or urgent costs. |
| Debt burden | Debt payments are manageable and APRs are controlled. | High-interest debt grows or minimum payments crowd out essentials. |
| Spending concentration | Essential costs leave room for savings and flexibility. | Housing, car, debt, or subscriptions dominate the budget. |
| Income stability | Income is predictable or buffered. | Variable income without cash reserve. |

## Budget Frameworks

### 50/30/20

Use for a fast, simple budget:

- 50% needs: housing, groceries, utilities, insurance, transport, minimum debt payments.
- 30% wants: dining out, entertainment, shopping, travel, subscriptions.
- 20% savings and extra debt payoff.

Notes:

- High-cost cities or low-income periods may make 50/30/20 unrealistic.
- If debt is urgent, some "wants" may need to move into extra debt payoff.
- If income is irregular, calculate using conservative average monthly income.

### Zero-Based Budget

Use when the user wants tight control:

- Assign every unit of income to a purpose.
- Categories include essentials, savings, discretionary, sinking funds, and debt payoff.
- Good for users with variable expenses, overspending, or specific goals.

### Pay-Yourself-First

Use when the user earns enough but fails to save:

- Automatically allocate savings on payday.
- Spend from the remaining amount.
- Works best with separate savings buckets.

### Envelope or Category Caps

Use when the user struggles with discretionary spending:

- Set category limits for food delivery, shopping, entertainment, rideshare, and hobbies.
- Track weekly, not just monthly.
- Move unused amounts to savings or debt payoff.

## Emergency Fund Planning

Common targets:

- Starter fund: one month of essential expenses.
- Standard fund: three to six months of essential expenses.
- Higher buffer: six to twelve months for variable income, dependents, unstable employment, medical risk, or single-income households.

Calculate:

```text
Emergency fund months = liquid savings / essential monthly expenses
```

Recommendations:

- Build a starter fund before aggressive extra debt payoff unless debt has extreme APR.
- Keep emergency funds liquid and low-risk.
- Do not count retirement accounts or volatile investments as immediate emergency cash.

## Savings Goal Planning

Inputs:

- Target amount.
- Current saved amount.
- Deadline.
- Monthly contribution.

Formula:

```text
Monthly contribution required = (target amount - current saved amount) / months until deadline
```

If the monthly contribution is unrealistic:

- Extend the timeline.
- Reduce the target.
- Identify category cuts.
- Add temporary income.
- Split into milestone goals.

## Debt Payoff Methods

### Avalanche

Prioritize highest APR debt first while paying minimums on all debts.

Best for:

- Minimizing total interest.
- Users who can stay motivated by long-term savings.

### Snowball

Prioritize smallest balance first while paying minimums on all debts.

Best for:

- Motivation.
- Users who need quick wins.

### Hybrid

Use when one debt has a very high APR, but another small debt can be eliminated quickly.

Warnings:

- Do not advise missing minimum payments.
- Do not recommend consolidation without discussing fees, APR, term length, and behavior risk.
- Flag urgent situations for a qualified debt counselor or financial professional.

## Spending Category Benchmarks

Benchmarks vary by region and income. Use as discussion prompts.

| Category | Rough Range | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Housing | 25% to 35% of income | High-cost areas may exceed this, but flexibility drops. |
| Food | 10% to 15% of income | Split groceries and restaurants if possible. |
| Transportation | 10% to 15% of income | Include fuel, public transit, insurance, maintenance, car payments. |
| Utilities | 5% to 10% of income | Include phone and internet if user groups them here. |
| Insurance | Varies | Health, auto, renters/home, life. |
| Debt payments | Below 15% preferred | High-interest debt needs priority. |
| Savings | 10% minimum target, 20% strong target | Adjust for income and debt. |

## Recommendation Patterns

Use this format:

```text
Action: Reduce [category] from [current] to [target].
Impact: Frees about [amount] per month.
Why: [category] is [percentage] of expenses and above the user's target/benchmark.
Tradeoff: [what user gives up].
Next step: [specific habit or tracking action].
```

Prioritize:

1. Correct data errors or missing categories.
2. Stop negative cash flow.
3. Protect essentials.
4. Build starter emergency fund.
5. Attack high-interest debt.
6. Automate savings.
7. Optimize discretionary categories.
8. Plan long-term goals.

## Professional Review Triggers

Recommend professional help when:

- User may be insolvent or unable to make minimum payments.
- User asks about bankruptcy, debt settlement, tax liens, wage garnishment, foreclosure, or eviction.
- User wants investment allocation, retirement account strategy, tax optimization, or insurance product selection.
- User has large medical debt, legal claims, divorce, inheritance, business debt, or cross-border tax issues.
- User is considering major debt consolidation or secured lending.
